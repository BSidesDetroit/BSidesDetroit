BSides is a community-run security conference model built around local people, local problems, and practical knowledge sharing. Every city gives it a different shape. BSides Detroit is our version: independent, volunteer-powered, and rooted in the Detroit technology and security community.

## What is a BSides?

Security BSides events started as an alternative space for conversations that did not always fit into larger conferences. They are usually smaller, more direct, and more community-driven. The hallway track matters. First-time speakers matter. Local stories matter.

At BSides Detroit, that means creating a place where defenders, builders, breakers, students, veterans, operators, researchers, and curious people can learn from each other without needing permission from the biggest room in the industry.

## Why join a community-run conference?

Because security is better when people know each other. A community-run conference is not only about talks and badges. It is about finding peers, asking better questions, sharing what actually happened, and making room for people who are still figuring out where they fit.

The work gets stronger when knowledge moves sideways through the community, not only downward from vendors, employers, or big stages.

## Why Detroit?

Detroit knows how to build, repair, improvise, and keep going. That history matters in technology. The city has deep roots in engineering, manufacturing, logistics, education, healthcare, mobility, art, and community organizing. Security touches all of it.

BSides Detroit exists because this region has people doing real work in real systems, and those people deserve a place to compare notes, sharpen skills, mentor others, and build trust.

## Operating principles

1. Community first.
2. Practical talks over vendor pitches.
3. Accessible by design.
4. Respectful by default.
5. Built by volunteers who care about Detroit.
